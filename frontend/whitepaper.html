Whitepaper · v1

The concentrated position that recenters itself.

Rangekeeper is a Uniswap v3 vault that is its own sole liquidity provider. It opens one concentrated-liquidity band and closes-then-reopens it nearer the live price as the market drifts — gated entirely by on-chain re-validation, not a keeper's word.

No proof is trusted. Only recomputed.

01

Introduction

Rangekeeper is not a strategy that sits beside a Uniswap v3 position — it is the position. A PairVault is the sole liquidity provider for its pair: one concentrated-liquidity band, sized entirely from real depositor capital, managed on-chain from open to close. There is no separate LP whose exposure it's hedging, and no off-chain custody of funds at any point.

Concentrated liquidity earns far higher fee density than a full-range position — at a cost. Once price drifts near either edge of the band, the position quietly converts to a single asset, stops earning, and starts eating impermanent loss with nothing left to offset it. The two usual fixes are to widen the range, giving up the efficiency concentration bought in the first place, or to babysit it by hand, which doesn't scale past one position and one attentive human.

Rangekeeper's answer is a vault that recenters its own band. An off-chain solver watches urgency and proposes when, but every recenter re-derives its own justification from on-chain state before it is allowed to move a cent of depositor capital — the trigger, a sanity check against the pool's real cached price, and a per-tier cooldown. A keeper can propose; it can never dictate.

The flows between them:

  • Traders ⇄ pool. Anyone can swap, no vault interaction needed. Every swap moves the pool's own price, readable at any time via its public slot0().
  • Pool → VaultManager. At the moment a recenter is proposed, VaultManager reads the pool's live slot0() directly. That live price is what the sanity check is measured against — not whatever a keeper's proof claims.
  • Keeper → VaultManager. The solver submits a recenter request carrying d_min/σ for its tier. VaultManager re-derives the trigger from these numbers itself; it never reads a boolean "should recenter" from off-chain.
  • ParamsRegistry → VaultManager. Every tier's risk thresholds — read fresh on every recenter, changeable only through a 2-day timelock.
  • VaultManager → PairVault. Once (and only once) both gates clear, VaultManager.recenter calls closeBand() then openBand() on the vault, atomically, in one transaction.
  • Depositors ⇄ PairVault. Deposit an asset for NAV-priced shares (optionally pushing a fresh Pyth update in the same transaction); withdraw a proportional claim on idle balances.

03

Recentering, step by step

  • 1. The vault opens its own band. Sized from its own depositor capital, under a risk tier whose parameters live in the timelocked ParamsRegistry. The very first band opens unconditionally — there is no "edge" to be near yet.
  • 2. An off-chain solver measures urgency. The loop reads live Pyth prices and realized volatility, computes the closed-form trigger locally (§04), and submits a recenter request with a proof once the tier's threshold is crossed.
  • 3. The chain re-derives the justification. VaultManager recomputes the trigger from the proof, confirms the band is genuinely near an edge using the pool's own live price (§05), and enforces a per-tier cooldown. A fabricated "urgent" proof for a centered band reverts.
  • 4. Recenter — close, then reopen, atomically. One transaction pushes a fresh Pyth update, closes the old band (principal and fees return to the vault), and opens a new one centered on the live price. The vault is never out of the pool for longer than a single block, and the move is NAV-neutral by construction.
  • 5. A circuit breaker for real volatility. If realized volatility is above the tier's ceiling, the band closes and does not reopen — capital sits in idle balances until a later call finds calmer conditions (§05).

04

The trigger: urgency without a CDF

Position-in-range measures where the live price sits between the band's own bounds, in sqrt-price terms (0 at the lower bound, 1 at the upper):

\( r \;=\; \dfrac{\sqrt{P} - \sqrt{P_a}}{\sqrt{P_b} - \sqrt{P_a}} \)
eq. 1 — RiskMath.positionInRange

The log-distance to the nearer edge, and urgency itself, follow directly:

\( d_{\min} = \min\!\big(\ln(P/P_a),\; \ln(P_b/P)\big) \qquad U = 2\,\Phi\!\left(-\dfrac{d_{\min}}{\sigma\sqrt{T}}\right) \)
eq. 2

A recenter fires once urgency U crosses the tier's probability threshold θ*. U is monotonically decreasing in d_min / (σ√T), so rather than evaluate a normal CDF (Φ) on-chain — real gas cost, real approximation error — θ* is converted off-chain, once per tier, into an equivalent z-score:

\( z^{*} = \Phi^{-1}\!\left(1 - \dfrac{\theta^{*}}{2}\right) \qquad \text{triggered} \iff \dfrac{d_{\min}}{\sigma\sqrt{T}} \le z^{*} \)
eq. 2 (contract form)

RiskMath.isTriggered only ever compares two already-scaled WAD numbers — zero CDF approximation risk, zero extra gas. Tier 1's queued parameters: zStar ≈ 0.67449 (θ* = 0.50), sigmaMax = 0.08, over a 3-day monitoring window.

05

The sanity check and the circuit breaker

A keeper's proof carries d_min and σ computed off-chain — numbers the chain can't independently recompute cheaply. So VaultManager cross-checks the proof against something it can verify cheaply: the pool's own live price, read directly via slot0() and run back through positionInRange, then tested against the tier's edge band directly, with no logarithm needed on-chain at all:

\( \text{withinEdgeBand}(r) \;=\; r \le r_{\text{low}} \;\;\lor\;\; r \ge r_{\text{high}} \)
RiskMath.withinEdgeBand

A recenter needs both gates: the proof's own trigger, and the chain's independent confirmation that the band is genuinely near an edge right now. A keeper can no longer fabricate an urgent-looking proof for a band the chain can see sitting comfortably centered — test/VaultManager.t.sol exercises exactly this rejection.

Above the tier's volatility ceiling (sigmaMax), a recenter still closes the band — but does not reopen it. Without care, that closed state could reopen unconditionally on the very next call regardless of whether conditions had actually calmed. VaultManager.paused[tier] avoids that by distinguishing two states that would otherwise look identical on-chain:

StateMeaningNext recenter
Never openedNo band has ever existed for this tierOpens unconditionally — nothing to be "near the edge" of yet
PausedClosed because volatility exceeded the ceilingRequires a later call to find calm conditions before it resumes

Regression guard: test_recenter_pausesOnHighVolatility_thenRequiresCalmToResume.

06

Fees: harvested, split, real

PairVault.collectFees() is a permissionless harvest: it calls the pool's own burn(tickLower, tickUpper, 0) — the standard zero-liquidity "poke" that moves accrued fees into the position's owed-tokens balance without freeing any principal — then collect()s the result into the vault's idle balances. test/PairVaultFees.t.sol verifies this against real fees from a real swap — not a mock, not a zero-fee no-op.

Every harvest splits three ways:

80%
to depositors, via NAV
15%
to the protocol treasury
5%
to whoever calls collectFees

The keeper rebate exists so the party actually paying gas to harvest fees is compensated for it; the remainder seeds protocol-owned liquidity later. This split applies only to a standalone collectFees() harvest — not to the principal-plus-fees a recenter's closeBand() returns, which would need per-position fee-growth snapshotting this version doesn't do. Flagged, not silently approximated.

07

Share pricing and the spare pocket

Deposits are priced against a real dual-asset NAV via Pyth, not credited 1:1. A deposit of amountRaw mints shares proportional to its Pyth-priced value:

\( \text{value} = \dfrac{\text{amountRaw} \times \text{priceWad}}{10^{\text{decimals}}} \qquad \text{shares} = \text{totalShares} \times \dfrac{\text{value}}{\text{NAV}} \)
PairVault._valueWad

navWad() sums three things per side, each Pyth-priced: idle balance (the "spare pocket"), whatever's swept to a yield source, and the live value of any currently-open band — via LiquidityMath.getAmountsForLiquidity against the pool's real current price. Most of a vault's value is expected to sit in its own band by design, so NAV has to price that band live rather than only the idle balances sitting alongside it. test_openBand_doesNotChangeNav is the regression guard that a recenter itself never silently moves NAV.

depositWithPriceUpdate(asset, amount, priceUpdateData) pushes a fresh Pyth update and prices the deposit atomically in the same transaction — the correct pattern for a pull oracle, since two separate transactions can't reliably land inside the 60-second staleness window once real block time and signing latency are counted. A side with zero balance is never priced at all, so a single-sided deposit into an empty vault never depends on the other asset's feed being fresh.

sweepIdleToYield() / sweepYieldToIdle() move up to 80% of idle balance (SWEEP_BPS) to and from a pluggable IYieldSource — a real Aave-V3-shaped adapter exists and is tested, including proportional interest accrual between depositors. It is unset (address(0)) on the live deployment today: Aave V3 is Monad-mainnet-only, and the one lending market that existed on Monad testnet has been retired by its own team. withdraw() auto-pulls from the yield source if local balance is short, so a depositor's claim is never stranded there.

08

The risk framework beyond v1

The math spec behind Rangekeeper covers more ground than VaultManager v1 actually reads on-chain. The following are implemented as tested, pure functions in RiskMath.sol today — verified in isolation, but not yet wired into the recenter gate. That's a deliberate v1 simplification, flagged here rather than left to be discovered: capped-injection sizing (kMax), a cumulative loss-coverage circuit breaker (lcrMin), and a trend-based stop (mStar) all remain declared fields on ParamsRegistry.TierParams that v1 does not read.

Concentration amplifies impermanent loss by a closed-form factor:

\( \gamma = \dfrac{1}{1 - \sqrt{P_a/P_b}} = \dfrac{\sqrt{P_b}}{\sqrt{P_b}-\sqrt{P_a}} \)
eq. 5
\( IL_{\text{full}}(\rho) = \dfrac{2\sqrt{\rho}}{1+\rho} - 1 \qquad IL_{\text{conc}} = \gamma \cdot IL_{\text{full}}(\rho) \)
eq. 6

where ρ is the current-to-entry price ratio. A tranche's loss-coverage ratio nets accrued fee yield against that loss:

\( LCR = 1 + f_t - |IL_{\text{conc}}| \)
eq. 7

and the price ratios at which a tranche would breach a given LCR_min follow in closed form, with no iteration needed on-chain:

\( A = 1 - \dfrac{c}{\gamma} \qquad \rho_{\uparrow} = \left(\dfrac{1+\sqrt{1-A^2}}{A}\right)^{2} \qquad \rho_{\downarrow} = \left(\dfrac{1-\sqrt{1-A^2}}{A}\right)^{2} \)
eq. 8

And when a band ever holds both protocol-owned and user liquidity side by side, fee income splits by contributed share, not evenly:

\( \text{protocolShare} = \text{totalFees} \times \dfrac{L_{\text{protocol}}}{L_{\text{protocol}} + L_{\text{user}}} \)
eq. 10 — RiskMath.feeSplit

All five functions are unit-tested against known values today. Wiring any of them into the live recenter gate is future work, not a promise made by this document.

Two tiers, live today

Each tier has exactly one vault, permanently, resolvable with a single vaultOfTier(tier) view call.

TierPairFee / tick spacingNote
Tier 1DMOB / DMOA (demo tokens)3000 / 60First band open, ticks [-600, 600]
Tier 2real testnet USDC / DMOA500 / 10The first vault whose value isn't entirely demo tokens
Pairing assets with different decimals

Tier 2 pairs real USDC (6 decimals) against DMOA (18 decimals). A raw Uniswap v3 pool price of 1.0 only means equal value when both assets share the same decimals — true for tier 1's two 18-decimal demo tokens, not true here. Since a v3 pool's price is fixed forever at initialize(), tier 2's pool is initialized at the decimal-adjusted 1:1 point instead — sqrtPriceX96 = 10^6 · 2^96 when USDC lands as token0, or its inverse when it doesn't (token order depends on the two addresses, decided only at deploy time) — derived from each token's own decimals rather than hardcoded either way, and verified afterward by reading the new pool's real slot0() directly rather than assumed.
